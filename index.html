Winter (2009) de Leva (1996), male de Leva (1996), female

The tables define segments differently. Winter's thigh starts at the greater trochanter and de Leva's at the hip joint centre. Winter's foot runs from malleolus to metatarsal and de Leva's from heel to toe. Winter's head row includes the neck from C7–T1, and de Leva's trunk runs from suprasternale to mid-hip.

Method

Two published tables are available. Choose the one that fits your subjects, and report which one you used.

For sagittal-plane analysis such as gait, use de Leva's transverse axis, which runs side to side. The sagittal axis runs front to back, and the longitudinal axis runs along the segment.

Starred trunk rows all use the greater trochanter to glenohumeral length, and their proximal end is the trochanter.

Limits of these tables

  • Both give population averages, not values for an individual. Where accuracy matters, measure the subject directly, for example with DXA, or use your own measured lengths.
  • Neither table covers children or adjusts for age. Do not apply them to children.
  • De Leva's female values rest on 15 subjects, and both tables have limited ethnic and body-type coverage.
  • Segment densities are fixed values from Winter's table. The variation with body density in Winter's Figure 4.2 is not modelled, because the book gives it only as a graph.
  • Scaling de Leva's lengths by height assumes the same body proportions at every height.
  • For education and research. Not a clinical or medical device.

Validation

Live checks against published worked examples and physical identities. De Leva's table was also compared with BMClab's independent transcription: all 210 shared values match.
